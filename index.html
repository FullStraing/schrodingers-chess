      Schrödinger's Chess

      A chess set whose pieces cannot remember what they are, played against an opponent who never got the rulebook.

      • Every turn, each of that player's pieces is randomly reassigned a way of moving. A piece keeps its face but not its habits.
      • No check, no checkmate. The king is an ordinary piece.
      • You each have one secret piece. Capture the other side's and you win instantly.
      • EGO starts knowing nothing — not how pieces move, not what wins. The notebook at the bottom of the page is it figuring that out while you play.

      How this works

      • Every turn is a fresh roll. The badge on a piece is what it moves as this turn.
      • Pawn one square forward, captures diagonally forward, no double step. Knight jumps. Bishop, rook, queen slide and can be blocked. King steps one square.
      • No check, no checkmate. Taking the king wins nothing.
      • Promotion applies to anything that still looks like a pawn, whatever it moved as to get there.
      • You win by capturing the opponent's secret piece — and you won't know you've done it until you have.
      • EGO gets a handful of yes/no questions per turn: "may I move this there?" It learns from the refusals as much as the permissions.
